Why it matters

Small Leaks Become Expensive Problems

A dripping fixture is easy to notice. A pinhole leak inside a wall, under a concrete slab or along a buried water line is not, and it can run for weeks or months. Over that time it soaks insulation and drywall, feeds mold, weakens flooring and quietly inflates your water bill.

The EPA's WaterSense program estimates that the average household loses close to 10,000 gallons of water a year to leaks, and that about one in ten homes leaks 90 gallons or more per day. Finding the source early is almost always cheaper than repairing the damage later. Know the signs

Seven Warning Signs of a Hidden Water Leak

Any one of these can have a harmless explanation. Two or more together are a good reason to investigate.

1

Unexplained high water bill

A bill that climbs with no change in habits is one of the most common early signals.

2

Running water sounds

Hissing or trickling when every fixture is off often points to a pressurized line leaking.

3

Damp spots and stains

Discolored ceilings, bubbling paint or soft, warped drywall mean moisture is getting in.

4

Musty odor or mold

A persistent damp smell, especially in basements and closets, suggests hidden moisture.

5

Low or uneven pressure

A sudden pressure drop can mean water is escaping before it reaches your taps.

6

Warm floor spots

A warm patch on a slab floor can indicate a leaking hot water line beneath it.

A seventh sign needs no guesswork at all: a water meter that keeps moving when nothing is running. That is the test below.

Try it yourself

The Five-Minute Water Meter Test

This simple check tells you whether water is moving through your pipes when it should not be.

  1. Shut everything offStop all faucets, appliances, irrigation and the ice maker.
  2. Read the meterNote the numbers or the small leak indicator dial.
  3. Wait 15 to 30 minutesUse no water at all during this time.
  4. Check againIf the reading changed or the dial spins, water is escaping somewhere.

After the leak is found

Repairs, Water Damage and Insurance

Finding the leak is step one. Next comes the repair, then drying out and restoring whatever got wet. Mold can begin to develop within a couple of days of persistent moisture, so quick action matters. If the damage is significant, water damage restoration helps return the property to a safe, dry condition.

Insurance coverage varies by policy and by whether the leak was sudden or gradual, so ask your insurer what applies before you start. Photos, dates and a written inspection report make a claim far easier. If you want help organizing that paperwork, see insurance claim assistance for leak damage.

Cold-weather tip: Idaho winters can freeze and split exposed pipes. Before temperatures drop, insulate pipes in garages and crawlspaces, disconnect outdoor hoses and make sure everyone in the household knows where the main shut-off valve is.

Quick answers

Leak Detection FAQs

How do I know if I have a hidden water leak?

Look for an unexplained rise in your water bill, the sound of running water when everything is off, damp or stained walls and ceilings, a musty smell, low water pressure, warm floor spots and a water meter that keeps moving when all fixtures are off.

How long does leak detection take?

Most inspections take about one to three hours, depending on the size of the property and how complex the suspected leak is.

Do technicians have to tear out walls to find a leak?

Usually not. Acoustic detectors, infrared cameras and moisture meters pinpoint the leak so that any opening is small and targeted.

Is emergency leak detection available 24/7 in Nampa?

Yes. Nampa Emergency Leak Detection lists availability 24 hours a day, 7 days a week for urgent leak problems.

Does homeowners insurance cover leak damage?

Coverage depends on your policy and on whether the leak was sudden or gradual. Document the damage with photos and a written inspection report, then ask your insurer what applies.

What should I do first if I find a leak?

Shut off the water at the main valve if the leak is active, move belongings away from wet areas, take photos for your records and call a leak detection professional.
